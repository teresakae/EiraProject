**1. If I contact SAPA 129, will the police come and arrest my husband?**
No, not automatically. SAPA 129 is a helpline run by KemenPPPA. It records your report and connects you with the women's and children's protection service (UPTD PPA) in your area, which can offer counselling, a health check, legal help and a safe place to stay. A criminal case only starts if a police report is made. You can make it yourself, or give a family member or another person written authority to make it for you (UU PKDRT Pasal 26). ([Kontan](https://nasional.kontan.co.id/news/sistem-sapa-129-diperkuat-untuk-optimalkan-perlindungan-perempuan-dan-anak); [UU 23/2004](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/40597/uu-no-23-tahun-2004))

**3. Is what my husband does actually a crime? He doesn't hit me every day.**
Yes. The law recognises four kinds of household violence: physical, psychological, sexual, and "penelantaran" (neglect) (Pasal 5). Hitting is physical violence (Pasal 6). Insults that leave you afraid, without confidence or feeling helpless are psychological violence (Pasal 7). Keeping you dependent by limiting or forbidding work so that you are under his control is a form of neglect (Pasal 9 ayat 2), and it carries up to 3 years in prison (Pasal 49). ([UU 23/2004](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/40597/uu-no-23-tahun-2004))

**4. Will my husband go to jail? Who will pay for the kids?** *(lawyer review needed)*
Not necessarily. The law sets maximum penalties, and the judge can choose a fine instead of prison. Physical violence carries up to 5 years, or up to 4 months for "light" violence between spouses (Pasal 44). Psychological violence carries up to 3 years, or 4 months for the light version (Pasal 45). Neglect carries up to 3 years (Pasal 49). A judge can also order him to stay away from you or attend counselling (Pasal 50). Since 2026, many cases with lower penalties can end in a formal "restorative justice" agreement instead of a trial, but only if you sign it. Reporting does not by itself stop his duty to support the family. If money is the main worry, the neglect offence and a maintenance claim in a divorce case are the legal tools, and a legal aid worker can explain them. ([UU 23/2004](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/40597/uu-no-23-tahun-2004); [Hukumonline, RJ in the new KUHAP](https://www.hukumonline.com/klinik/a/restorative-justice-dalam-kuhap-baru-lt519065e9ed0a9))

**5. If I report, do I have to divorce him?**
No. A criminal report and a divorce are two separate processes. You can report and stay married. You can also file for divorce (gugat cerai at the Pengadilan Agama) without a police report. If you do want a divorce and can't afford it, you can ask to file for free (prodeo) with a certificate of low income (SKTM) from your kelurahan. Without prodeo it costs roughly Rp1.2 million. **6. If we divorce, will I lose my children?**
For Muslim families, the Kompilasi Hukum Islam (KHI) starts from the position that children under 12 stay with their mother, and the father pays for their upkeep (KHI Pasal 105). This is not automatic. The judge decides what is best for the child and can rule otherwise if a mother neglects her duties. A wife who files for divorce can also ask for nafkah iddah and mut'ah, as long as she is not found to be nusyuz (SEMA 3/2018). In practice, getting the husband to actually pay can be hard. ([Marinews MA, hadhanah](https://marinews.mahkamahagung.go.id/artikel/bukan-mutlak-milik-ibu-memahami-kriteria-pencabutan-hak-0F8); [Marinews MA, mut'ah and nafkah iddah](https://marinews.mahkamahagung.go.id/artikel/mutah-dan-nafkah-iddah-dalam-cerai-gugat-0bn8))

**7. Can I be protected from him right away?** *(lawyer review needed)*
The law says police must give you temporary protection within 24 hours of receiving a report, and that protection lasts up to 7 days (Pasal 16). Within another 24 hours, the police must ask the court for a protection order. The court chief must issue it within 7 days (Pasal 28), and it can last up to 1 year, with extensions (Pasal 32). You, your family, a friend, a companion or the police can apply, orally or in writing, at the district court (Pasal 29–30). If he breaks the order, police can arrest him without a warrant (Pasal 35). In practice these orders seem to be rarely used and responses vary, so ask your companion to push for one. ([UU 23/2004](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/40597/uu-no-23-tahun-2004))

> **RESEARCH UPDATE (AI research, not lawyer-reviewed. Team to approve)**
>
> *Proposed text:* The law says police must give temporary protection within 24 hours of knowing about the violence or receiving a report, and that protection lasts up to 7 days (Pasal 16). Within 24 hours of giving it, police must ask the court for a protection order. The court chief must issue it within 7 days, unless there is a proper reason not to (Pasal 28).
