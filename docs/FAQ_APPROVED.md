## 1. If I contact SAPA 129, will the police come and arrest my husband?

Approved text: No, not automatically. SAPA 129 is a helpline run by KemenPPPA. It records your report and connects you with the women's and children's protection service (UPTD PPA) in your area, which can offer counselling, a health check, legal help and a safe place to stay. The national service standard says staff should explain the process and ask for your consent to a service plan. It also says that if a situation looks high-risk, staff may work with police, neighbourhood officials (RT/RW) or security officers to keep you safe, and they may come to where you are. You can ask the counsellor what they would do in your situation, and you can tell them the safest way and time to reach you. A police report is the usual way a criminal case starts. You can make it yourself, or give a family member or another person authority to make it for you (UU PKDRT Pasal 26). Other people can also contact SAPA 129 or the police, and for offences that do not need your complaint, the law lets police act once they know about it (see answer 11).

## 2. Can I report without giving my name?

Approved text: The SAPA 129 website, relaunched in 2025, has an anonymous option, and SAPA says it keeps the reporter's identity confidential. We could not find a rule on how far that anonymity goes. The law gives you the right to have your case handled confidentially (UU PKDRT Pasal 10 huruf c), and the national service standard lists privacy and confidentiality as working principles. To get help like a visum, shelter or a companion, you will likely need to give your identity to the service. Its standard intake asks for details such as your name, address and contact number. Confidential does not mean he can never find out. If a court case starts, he would normally learn about it: he would be the other party in a divorce, and a criminal case or a protection order also involves him. We don't know for certain whether or when the service would contact him. Ask the counsellor before you agree to anything.

## 3. Is what my husband does actually a crime? He doesn't hit me every day.

Approved text: The law describes four kinds of household violence: physical, psychological, sexual, and "penelantaran" (neglect) (Pasal 5). The law sets penalties for these acts in general. These descriptions do not say an act has to happen often or every day. Physical violence is described as an act that causes pain, illness or serious injury (Pasal 6). Psychological violence is described as an act that causes fear, loss of confidence, loss of the ability to act, helplessness or severe psychological suffering (Pasal 7). Sexual violence includes forcing sexual relations on someone living in the household (Pasal 8). Neglect includes not supporting someone a person has a legal duty to support, and keeping someone dependent by limiting or forbidding proper work so that she is under that person's control (Pasal 9). For neglect, the law sets a maximum of 3 years in prison or a fine (Pasal 49). The other kinds have their own maximums (see answer 4). Whether a particular situation meets these descriptions is decided by the police, prosecutors and courts, not by this app. A legal aid worker can look at your situation with you.

## 4. Will my husband go to jail? Who will pay for the kids?

Approved text: Not necessarily, and this app can't say what would happen in any one case. The law sets maximum penalties, and the judge can choose a fine instead of prison. Physical violence carries up to 5 years, with higher maximums for more serious harm, or up to 4 months for "light" violence between spouses (Pasal 44). Psychological violence carries up to 3 years, or 4 months for the light version (Pasal 45). Forced sexual relations in the household carry up to 12 years (Pasal 46). Neglect carries up to 3 years (Pasal 49). A judge can also order a person who is convicted to stay away from the victim or attend counselling (Pasal 50). A person can be held before trial only for some offences. The new KUHAP generally allows it for offences with a maximum of 5 years or more, plus a listed set, and it also asks for evidence and a further reason such as a risk of fleeing (Pasal 100). As we read it, the 3-year and 4-month offences above are not on that list. Since 2026, a case can sometimes end in a formal "restorative justice" agreement instead of continuing. It is meant for a first offence with a maximum of up to 5 years, and never for sexual violence (KUHAP Pasal 80, 82). It needs your signature, and the law says it must be free of pressure, threats or deception (Pasal 81, 83). If what was agreed is not done, the case can continue (Pasal 79). The detailed government rules were still pending the last time we could confirm. A husband's duty to support the family is set by marriage law (UU 1/1974 Pasal 34), and reporting does not by itself end it. We found nothing on what actually happens to family income if a husband is held or jailed, so that is a fair question for a legal aid worker. If money is the main worry, the neglect offence and a maintenance claim are the legal tools. A maintenance claim can be made in a divorce case. Marriage law also says a spouse who neglects these duties can be taken to court, so ask a legal aid worker whether that is possible without a divorce.

## 5. If I report, do I have to divorce him?

Approved text: No. A criminal report and a divorce are two separate processes. You can report and stay married. You can also file for divorce (gugat cerai at the Pengadilan Agama) without a police report. If you do want a divorce and can't afford it, you can ask to file for free (prodeo) with a certificate of low income (SKTM) from your kelurahan. Without prodeo, one legal guide estimates roughly Rp1.2 million, but there is no fixed amount and it differs by court. The same guide says a first-level case takes up to about 6 months. We found no figures for how long cases really take in Bekasi. If the reason given is constant quarrelling, Supreme Court guidance generally asks that the couple have lived apart for at least 6 months, unless household violence is found as a fact in the case. Other grounds exist, including cruelty or serious abuse (KHI Pasal 116). A legal aid worker can explain which ground might apply. This answer is for Muslim couples. If you are not Muslim, the process is different and this answer doesn't cover it.

## 6. If we divorce, will I lose my children?

Approved text: For Muslim families, the Kompilasi Hukum Islam (KHI) starts from the position that children under 12 stay with their mother, and the father pays for their upkeep (KHI Pasal 105). An older child who is able to decide may choose (Pasal 105, 156). This is not automatic. The judge decides what is best for the child and can rule otherwise, for example if a parent cannot keep the child physically and spiritually safe (Pasal 156). The father's duty to pay depends on his ability and lasts at least until the child is 21 (Pasal 156). A wife who files for divorce can also ask for nafkah iddah and mut'ah, as long as she is not found to be nusyuz (SEMA 3/2018). In practice, getting the husband to actually pay can be hard. If you are not Muslim, different rules apply and this answer doesn't cover them.

## 7. Can I be protected from him right away?

Approved text: The law says police must give temporary protection within 24 hours of knowing about the violence or receiving a report, and that protection lasts up to 7 days (Pasal 16). Within 24 hours of giving it, police must ask the court for a protection order. The court chief must issue it within 7 days, unless there is a proper reason not to (Pasal 28). It can last up to 1 year, with extensions (Pasal 32). The police do not have to be the ones who apply. You, your family, a friend, a companion, a spiritual adviser or the police can apply, orally or in writing, at the district court (Pasal 29-30). If someone else applies, they generally need your consent, though the law allows exceptions in certain circumstances (Pasal 30). If an order is broken, police can arrest the person who broke it without a warrant (Pasal 35). That is what the law says. In practice, women's legal aid groups and researchers report that temporary protection is often not given within 24 hours and that court orders are rarely issued. We found no official count. A companion or a legal aid worker can ask for protection with you. A shelter may be possible before any court order (see answer 12).

## 8. What proof do I need? It's my word against his.

Approved text: The law says a victim's testimony can be enough if there is one other valid piece of evidence (UU PKDRT Pasal 55). Police, prosecutors and the judge still decide whether something is proved. That other piece could be a medical report or visum (which police request from a hospital or puskesmas, Pasal 21), a psychologist's report, a witness, photos of injuries, chat messages, or records showing that someone controls the money or forbids you to work. Some women keep a dated diary (kronologi). If you keep one, keep it somewhere he can't find it. Whether a recording of a conversation you are part of can be used is not settled. Some legal writers say it can, others read a 2016 Constitutional Court ruling more narrowly, and the new KUHAP says evidence obtained unlawfully cannot be used (Pasal 235). Ask a legal aid worker before relying on a recording. Don't install spyware or read his phone, because that may itself be an offence. Sharing recordings or accusations publicly can also bring legal risk, and Komnas Perempuan has recorded wives facing counter-reports. In practice, some police still ask for extra witnesses. If that happens, a legal aid worker can help you point to Pasal 55.

## 9. Does any of this cost money? I have no income.

Approved text: Calling SAPA 129 is free. The national service standard says UPTD PPA services should not be charged to you, and that staff may not ask you for payment. The law gives you the right to health care, a companion, and legal aid at every stage, as the rules provide (Pasal 10). The visum is less certain. The standard lists the visum as a cost a regional service can budget for, and in 2026 a deputy minister said it is budgeted but that there are problems on the ground. Reports from 2026 describe victims being charged in some areas. Amounts reported range from about Rp150,000 to around Rp1 million. Sources also disagree on whether BPJS covers it, and some say it does not. We don't know the position in Bekasi. Ask before the examination whether you would have to pay, and ask UPTD PPA or LBH APIK for help if you are charged.

## 10. Will the police just tell us to make peace?

Approved text: It happens often, but you don't have to agree. Women's legal aid groups report that police often push for "damai" or turn reports away, especially in neglect cases. Under the new KUHAP (2026), a peace agreement (keadilan restoratif) must be signed by you, and the law says it must be made without pressure, threats or deception (Pasal 81, 83). That is the rule on paper, and women's groups worry about pressure in practice. If what was agreed is not done, the case can continue (Pasal 79). Sexual violence can never be settled this way (UU TPKS Pasal 23; KUHAP Pasal 82). UPTD PPA may also offer mediation. The national service standard says that is your choice and your decision, and that an agreement does not cancel the other person's legal responsibility. The new KUHAP also says police should give you proof of a report, and that if there is no response within 14 days you can go to the investigator's superior (Pasal 23). It helps to bring a companion from UPTD PPA or LBH APIK to the police station.

## 11. What if I change my mind and want to withdraw?

Approved text: It depends on the offence. "Light" spousal violence and marital sexual violence (Pasal 44(4), 45(2) and 46, with Pasal 51-53) are complaint offences, so they only go ahead if you complain. Two time limits in the new KUHP appear to apply. A complaint can be made within 6 months of the day you learned of the offence (Pasal 29). You can withdraw it within 3 months of making it, but you can't file the same complaint again (Pasal 30). We found no source applying these limits to household violence cases by name, and we don't know how the 6 months is counted when things happen more than once. A legal aid worker can tell you how this might apply. We mention it so you know it exists, not to hurry you. More serious harm and neglect are not complaint offences, so a case can go ahead without your complaint. Police and prosecutors decide which offence fits, so you may not know at first which rule applies. Once police are involved, withdrawing doesn't automatically stop the case, although a restorative justice agreement might. In that process, the law says a report can be withdrawn only after the agreement has been fully carried out (KUHAP Pasal 79). Talk this through with a legal aid worker before you decide.

## 12. Can I and my children go somewhere safe?

Approved text: The law says social workers should take victims to a safe house (rumah aman) or other housing (Pasal 22). UPTD PPA can offer temporary shelter after an assessment, and you would be asked to sign a consent form. The national service standard sets a maximum of 14 days in a UPTD PPA temporary shelter. After that, a person may be referred to another safe house, a social institution or LPSK. Shelter can be given before any court protection order. The standard covers both women and children, but we found no clear rule on whether your children can come with you. Availability differs by area, and some areas have no shelter, so it depends on where you live. SAPA 129 or the UPTD PPA in your city or regency can tell you what exists there. Whether to use any of this is your decision.
